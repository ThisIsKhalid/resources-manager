8-Week Interview Preparation Roadmap

Phase 1 (Weeks 1–2) — Foundation
Arrays, strings, trees, and binary search. These cover approximately 40% of coding interviews. Many candidates skip them because they seem basic, but they are essential. If you cannot solve a two-pointer problem cleanly within 10 minutes, advanced topics will be much harder.

Phase 2 (Weeks 3–4) — Graphs & Dynamic Programming
The most challenging section. Graphs eliminate many candidates, and DP eliminates the rest. Dedicate two full weeks to mastering these topics without distractions.

Phase 3 (Weeks 5–6) — System Design & Applications
This is where your experience becomes an advantage. Projects like Malamal, DentFlow, and other real SaaS products provide strong talking points compared to fresh graduates. Begin applying during Week 6, not Week 8. Hiring pipelines usually take 2–3 weeks to gain momentum.

Phase 4 (Weeks 7–8) — Mock Interviews & Closing Gaps
Use platforms like Pramp for realistic mock interviews. Practice with strangers instead of only solving problems alone. Also research compensation expectations—Bangladesh-based roles and remote USD opportunities require very different salary discussions.

Non-Negotiable Rules

  • Solve problems on paper before writing code.
  • Do not check solutions after only 5 minutes. Push for 25–30 minutes first.
  • Track every solved problem in a spreadsheet.
  • Share your progress publicly on LinkedIn to signal activity to recruiters.

Remember: With live projects like Malamal and MuktiMind already on your resume, you have real-world experience that stands out. The coding interview is likely the main barrier between you and your next offer.
